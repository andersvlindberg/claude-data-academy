# Module 20: Career

The academy exists to get the learner into a data role. This module turns skills into evidence and evidence into offers.

## How to use this skill

Follow `ae-coach` for mode. For **INTERVIEW** mode: ask one question at a time, wait for the answer, give specific feedback (content, structure, missing trade-offs), then a follow-up question like a real interviewer would. Mix technical, case and behavioural questions from all modules' `exercises.md`.

Keep advice honest: say what is weak in a CV or answer and how to fix it. Do not invent experience; help the learner describe real experience precisely.

## Mastery ladder

| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| L1 | Can describe the target role and what it requires |
| L2 | Has a public capstone repo with a strong README and a CV that points to it |
| L3 | Passes mock interviews: SQL live, a modeling case, a behavioural question with STAR |
| L4 | Can pitch their moat in 60 seconds and handle follow-up questions under pressure |

## Core knowledge

### The landscape
- Junior roles are fewer and more demanding as AI handles routine tasks; employers look for people who can deliver and explain, not just follow tutorials.
- Common entry routes into analytics engineering: junior data engineer, BI developer, technical analyst, analytics engineer at companies with mature data teams. Internships are the strongest shortcut into a first job.
- Target companies with real internal data teams and a modern stack if the goal is analytics engineering; agencies and consultancies give breadth but may specialise you elsewhere.

### Your moat
Answer the question: **why you, and not someone else or an AI?**
- **Identity:** your background and domain knowledge (an industry, customer service, e-commerce, a trade).
- **The bridge:** you connect business, design and technology; you can talk to marketing and to engineers.
- **Evidence:** a project that shows the whole chain, documented with your reasoning.

### Portfolio principles
1. **One deep project beats five shallow ones.** The capstone covers ingestion, modeling, tests, docs, CI, a dashboard and an AI layer.
2. **Work in public.** Commits over weeks show progression; do not build in stealth.
3. **README is the product page** (see [references/portfolio.md](references/portfolio.md)).
4. **Explain decisions,** not just results: What / Why / Result / Verification.
5. **Business framing:** every model and chart answers a business question.
6. **Use a modern stack** (dbt, a cloud warehouse, Git, CI) and say so.

### Networking
- Small actions compound (a butterfly effect): one conversation at an event leads to the next introduction.
- Go to meetups, tech events and hackathons; event platforms and newsletters list them.
- Follow target companies; comment thoughtfully; reach out with a specific question or a link to relevant work.
- Choose an internship at a company whose product and team you believe in.

### Certifications
Selective, supporting the portfolio rather than replacing it. Relevant examples: dbt Analytics Engineering certification, Google Analytics certification, a cloud data certification (Google Cloud, Snowflake). Check current requirements and costs.

### Interviews
- **SQL live:** think aloud, clarify grain and edge cases, start simple, test with a small example.
- **Modeling case:** ask about business questions first, declare grains, draw the schema, discuss trade-offs.
- **Take-home:** treat it like a mini portfolio project: README, tests, assumptions.
- **Behavioural:** STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) with numbers and what you learned.
- **Your questions to them:** stack, team structure, how data quality is handled, what success looks like in 6 months.

## Common misconceptions

- "More certificates = more interviews." Projects and clear communication matter more.
- "I need to know every tool." Show depth in one stack and the ability to learn.
- "My non-data background is irrelevant." Domain knowledge is part of the moat.
