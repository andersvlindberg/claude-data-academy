# Course notes: GDPR in customer data and surveys (my summary)

## Seven principles (Art. 5)
Lawfulness, fairness and transparency · purpose limitation · data minimisation · accuracy · storage limitation · integrity and confidentiality · accountability.

## Surveys and customer research
- **Necessity:** only collect what the purpose needs. If the purpose is to understand why customers leave, you do not automatically need name, email, health or financial situation.
- **Transparency:** tell respondents the purpose, how answers are used and who has access.
- **Consent forms** and digital consent in online surveys; document consent; handle withdrawal.
- **Anonymity versus confidentiality:** anonymous = no one can link answers to a person; confidential = the organisation can, but promises not to disclose. Anonymisation techniques: remove identifiers, aggregate, avoid small groups.
- **Protection:** encryption, access control, data handling policy, retention and deletion, training.

## Worked exercise pattern: assess a data collection
1. Assess the collection: keep what the purpose needs (e.g. answers about the service), question the rest (name, email, gender, health, finances).
2. GDPR risks: excessive data, sensitive categories, unclear purpose, broad access.
3. Measures: collect only what is needed; anonymise or pseudonymise; inform clearly; limit access; set retention.
4. Conclusion: decide the purpose first, then the data needed. Not "collect as much as possible and hope it becomes useful later".

## Consequences of breaches
Fines up to 20 million euro or 4% of global turnover; loss of trust and reputation; loss of customers; audits, restructuring, damages. Prevention: training, privacy by design, documentation, regular review.
