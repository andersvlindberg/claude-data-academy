# Course notes: customer journey and customer journey analytics (my summary)

## What a customer journey is
A picture of how the customer experiences the company over time: events, interactions, touchpoints, and the customer's feelings and situations. Purpose: deliver the right content in the right stage.

Formats: **subway map** (stages and lines) and **customer journey map** (stages × rows). Common splits: **3 phases** (before, during, after purchase) or **6 steps** (e.g. need, search, compare, buy, use, loyalty/recommend). Everyday example: going to the cinema, booking a flight.

When to use the map: to go from insight to action, align teams, prioritise efforts. **Which journeys first?** Start where you have the mandate to change things, where problems are known and where many customers are affected.

## Touchpoints and channels
A touchpoint is any point where customer and company meet. Examples by phase:
- **Before:** social media marketing, display ads, review sites, word of mouth, events, search.
- **During:** website, store, sales staff, checkout, booking, product demo, chat.
- **After:** satisfaction surveys, newsletter, support, invoices, warranty returns, loyalty.
A **channel** is the medium the touchpoint uses (web, app, store, phone, email).

## Customer journey analytics (CJA)
Analysis of customer data across touchpoints over time to understand and improve the journey, combining data from web, app, CRM, stores and service.
- **Moment of truth (MOT):** the moments that decide the customer's judgement (first use, first problem, delivery).
- **Pain points:** problems and frustrations. **Churn-risk moments:** when the risk of leaving is high (long waits, failed delivery, unresolved ticket).
- Measure experience with **NPS** (0–10, promoters 9–10, passives 7–8, detractors 0–6) and **CSAT** after interactions.

Step by step:
1. Define the journey's stages (awareness, consideration, purchase, post-purchase).
2. Identify touchpoints (social ads, site, app, customer service).
3. Collect data (social platforms, analytics, e-commerce, service system).
4. Segment customers (interest, geography, behaviour, value).
5. Analyse and find insights (events, drop-offs, patterns).
6. Optimise and test (A/B, new content, process changes).

Typical pitfalls: mapping the company's view instead of the customer's; too many journeys at once; no data behind the map; no owner for actions. Tips: start small, use real data and real customer voices, connect each insight to a KPI, revisit the map regularly.
