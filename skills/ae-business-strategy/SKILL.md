# Module 25: Business acumen and data-driven strategy

Analytics engineering only matters if it serves the business. This module gives the business vocabulary and frameworks to understand **why** a company needs certain data, and to speak with management in their language.

## How to use this skill

Follow `ae-coach` for mode. Without it: explain → LEARN, analyse/apply → BUILD, graded work → LEARN only (use a different company than the assignment; review their own canvas or goals).

Use well-known companies as worked examples (IKEA, a discount retailer, a streaming service, a car maker) and apply every framework to data: *which data does this strategy need, and which decisions does it support?*

## Core knowledge

### Organisations and businesses
- An **organisation** has structure, purpose and goals, collaboration, resources and culture; types include companies, authorities, non-profits and schools.
- **Why companies exist:** create economic value, meet needs and wants, create jobs, drive innovation, contribute to growth and society (taxes), meet owners' and stakeholders' goals (increasingly balancing economic, environmental and social goals).
- **Business (verksamhet):** the activities an organisation performs to reach its goals.
- **Business acumen (affärsmannaskap):** spotting opportunities, thinking strategically, understanding finances and customers, managing risks, and making decisions that create value.

### Digital versus data-driven
- **Digital:** using technology to improve the business, interact online, and use digital tools.
- **Data-driven:** decisions are based on data analysis, data shapes strategy, and strategies adapt continuously based on data. Being digital is necessary but not sufficient.
- **Not data-driven:** decisions on gut feeling or hierarchy, data in silos, reports nobody acts on.
- **Path to data maturity:** focus on data collection, inventory of tools and skills, commitment (data available to people), data-driven ideas at all levels (break silos), continuous improvement.
- **Data-driven way of working:** organisation (culture) → tools and technology → data collection and analysis → reporting → action, repeated.

### Strategic foundations
| Element | Question | Example (furniture retailer) |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Why do we exist (beyond profit)? | Create a better everyday life for many people |
| Business idea | What do we offer, to whom, how, and why do they choose us? | Affordable, functional home furnishing, self-service, sold online and in stores |
| Vision | Where do we want to be long term? | One of the Nordic region's leading home furnishing retailers |
| Mission | What do we do every day to get there? | Make good design affordable and easy to buy |
| Value proposition | Why should customers choose us? | Price, range, availability, design |
| Target group | Who exactly? | Price-conscious households; small businesses as secondary segment |

Purpose = why the company exists; business idea = how it does it in practice; goals = concrete results; vision = the desired future state. Each guides **which data to collect and analyse**.

**Personas** describe a typical customer (name, situation, needs, challenges, channels) so teams design and measure for real behaviour. Combine with data; do not invent personas without evidence.

### Business models and the Business Model Canvas
A business model describes how a company creates, delivers and captures value. Common models: e-commerce (direct, marketplace), subscription, platform/marketplace, freemium, advertising-funded, licensing.

**Business Model Canvas** (nine blocks):
1. Customer segments · 2. Value propositions · 3. Channels · 4. Customer relationships · 5. Revenue streams · 6. Key resources · 7. Key activities · 8. Key partners · 9. Cost structure.
Data lens: each block produces and needs data (segments → customer data; channels → channel performance; relationships → CRM; revenue → transactions; costs → cost data).

### Goals and goal hierarchy
- **Business goal:** where do we want to be as a company?
- **Strategic goals:** which routes take us there? (long term, several years)
- **Tactical goals:** which sub-goals and initiatives do our strategies need? (months to a year)
- **Operational goals:** what must teams achieve day to day or week to week?
- **Activities:** what do we actually do?
Goal model: **Vision → business goals → strategic → tactical → operational → activities.**

**SMART:** Specific, Measurable, Achievable (accepted), Relevant (realistic), Time-bound. Example: "Increase online repeat purchase rate from 22% to 26% by 31 December 2027."
**OKR** (Objectives and Key Results) is a common alternative: an inspiring objective with 2–4 measurable key results per quarter.

### KPIs
- **Measure:** anything measured, without a value judgement ("number of customers").
- **Metric:** combines at least two measures and indicates whether we move toward a goal ("customers per store").
- **KPI:** a metric linked to a goal and its context, used to steer.
- KPIs are used to **engage** (everyone pulls the same way), **control** (follow development), **learn** (experiment, measure, adjust) and **reward** (link goals and incentives).
- Types: **strategic** (management, long term) and **operational/functional** (teams). **Leading** indicators are proactive and predict outcomes (sales calls made, open tickets); **lagging** indicators report results (revenue, NPS).
- Ten common e-commerce KPIs: sales, conversion rate, AOV, new customers, returning customers, leads, CSAT, loyalty rate, CAC, website traffic.

**The KPI questions:** Which goal is it linked to? Who benefits from it? Which question does it answer? How often is data collected? How often is it reported? Where does the data come from? Who is responsible? What is the target and threshold?

**Is it a KPI?** Daily website visitors (a measure; KPI only if linked to a goal), background colour (no), online conversion rate (yes, if linked to a sales goal), browser type (no, a dimension), average time on site (rarely a good KPI on its own), logo design (no).

**Prioritise KPIs:** not every KPI can be top priority; rank by link to the most important goal, influence (can we act on it?), and data reliability. Then: prioritise → establish (definition, owner, dashboard) → follow up and decide.

### Creating business value with data
Five capabilities: collect and store data in a structured way; use the right tools and techniques (BI, analysis); have the right skills; integrate analysis across the organisation; protect data securely. Each must be tied to a measurable benefit.

### Decision making with data
- Frame the decision first (what will we do differently depending on the answer?).
- Combine data with judgement; be explicit about uncertainty and assumptions.
- Avoid common biases: confirmation bias, anchoring, survivorship, HiPPO (highest paid person's opinion).
- Decide, then **follow up**: without follow-up there is no learning.

### Leadership in data-informed organisations
Leaders model data use, ask for evidence, accept being wrong, protect time for analysis, and make ownership clear (data owner, steward, custodian). Psychological safety matters: people must be able to bring bad news from the data.

## Common misconceptions

- "Data-driven means the dashboard decides." Data informs; people decide and are accountable.
- "More KPIs give more control." Fewer, prioritised KPIs give focus.
- "Vision and mission are fluff." They decide which data matters.
- "A SMART goal is any goal with a number." It also needs relevance and a deadline.
